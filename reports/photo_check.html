Where the work photos are taken

Every finished MGNREGA work needs a geotagged photo as proof. We read the location of all 824,146 completion photos the government accepted in Karnataka for 2024-25 and asked one question: was the photo taken near the panchayat the work belongs to?

What these numbers mean, and what they do not
  • They do not show that any work is fake. They show where the government's own location check tells us little, or where something unusual is happening and the records are worth asking for.
  • There are innocent explanations. Labourers from several panchayats can be sent to one big tank, so many works get photographed there. A panchayat with two villages far apart can look "far" from its own middle point. A phone without a GPS fix can report an old location.
  • The check meant to catch problems almost never says no: Karnataka's moderators rejected 419 of 42.5 lakh after-photos (0.010%), a quarter of the national rate (Bhuvan statistics report).
  • Right to Information requests for the estimates and measurement books behind the highest-ranked blocks are being prepared. We will publish what comes back, including any explanation from the officials concerned.

Two measures to treat with care

Same exact GPS point. In some blocks many different works carry the identical location, to about a metre. Where we looked, the photos showed different places: the phone's location had stuck or been set by hand, rather than photos being reused. That still matters, because for those works the geotag proves nothing, and the system accepted it.

Phone "moving" faster than 60 km/h between photos. This is high across the whole north-east of the state (Yadgir, Raichur, Vijayapura). A pattern that broad is more likely wrong phone clocks or photos uploaded in batches than anything in one block.

How to check this yourself

  1. Open the Bhuvan-BHUGRAM dashboard. Choose the report "Accepted Geotags", stage "PHASE II: AFTER", state Karnataka, your district and block, and dates 01-04-2024 to 31-03-2025.
  2. Each row gives the work code, panchayat, location and two photo links. The photo's file name contains the phone's ID and the time it was taken.
  3. Group the photos by panchayat, take the middle location, and measure each photo's distance from it.

Method notes: blocks with fewer than 200 works are left out of the rankings but kept in the table. A "spot" is a cell of about 50 m. Coordinates outside Karnataka's bounding box are dropped. The calculation reproduces figures first computed from the dashboard's own Excel export for Koppal district.
