# How we look for hidden wealth: concealment methods and the traces they leave

Investigators (Income Tax, ED, CBI, Lokayukta) start from how money is usually hidden, then look for the trace each method leaves. We do the same, but only with public records: affidavits, company filings, court and audit records, government orders and tender results. A trace is a **lead**, never proof. Every lead is checked against original documents and put to the person before anything is published. Where a lead points at private individuals or at property that can only be examined with legal powers, it goes to the authorities as a complaint, not into a video.

These methods are all described in public enforcement reports (Income Tax and ED annual reports, FATF typologies, CAG audits, court judgments).

| # | How money is commonly hidden | Trace it can leave in public records | Where we look |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Land bought in the name of relatives, employees or associates (benami) | The politician's own declared land stops growing while linked trusts, firms or office-holding relatives acquire land; sale deeds registered far below guidance value | Affidavits across elections; trust and firm filings; Kaveri encumbrance certificates (owner downloads) |
| 2 | Small "shell" companies or LLPs receiving large sums | Tiny paid-up capital but large unsecured loans, "advances", share-application money or high share premiums | MCA master data and filings (Forms 8/11, AOC-4, MGT-7) |
| 3 | Loans from friendly non-bank companies, or unnamed "creditors" | Affidavit liabilities owed to companies that are not banks, or to unnamed "sundry creditors" | Affidavits (liabilities section); MCA records of the lender |
| 4 | Passing other income off as farm income (agricultural income is tax-exempt) | Declared agricultural income out of proportion to declared acreage | Affidavit income section vs land section |
| 5 | Educational or charitable trusts (cash fees and donations, land bought by the trust) | Trust land holdings and government land grants to the trust; family members as trustees | Trust websites and filings; government orders; CAG |
| 6 | Cash parked in cooperative banks or credit societies | Large deposits in cooperative societies or credit societies | Affidavits (movable assets) |
| 7 | Revaluation and undervalued purchases | Property bought at a token or zero cost; sudden value jumps on old land | Affidavit purchase cost vs current value, across elections |
| 8 | "Advances" for land deals that never complete | Large advances to firms or individuals carried for years | Affidavits (loans and advances given); recipient's filings |
| 9 | Firms linked to office-holders winning government work | Contracts or approvals to firms whose directors or partners are office-holders or their declared family | Karnataka e-Procurement award data; government orders; MCA directors |
| 10 | Explaining wealth as gifts from relatives | Large "gifts" declared, especially of land | Affidavit entries marked gift |
| 11 | Offshore companies and accounts | Names in leaked offshore databases with matching addresses or companies | ICIJ Offshore Leaks (checked Nov 2026: no match for any sitting MLA) |
| 12 | Unregistered partnership firms | Firms named in affidavits that appear in no registry | Affidavits vs MCA |

## Rules

- Public office-holders and registered entities only. We do not investigate private individuals' homes, land or lives; that is for agencies with legal powers.
- A pattern goes to the authorities (Income Tax Benami Prohibition Unit, Lokayukta, ED complaint) with documents attached, or to an established newsroom. It is published only when the documents prove the fact itself, with the person's reply.
- Every number is re-derived from the original document by a second, independent check.

## Investigators' tests we apply (public-record versions)

1. **Net worth test** (the method that convicted Al Capone): growth in declared net worth (assets minus liabilities) between two affidavits, set against the total income the person and spouse declared to the Income Tax department for the years in between (listed in the affidavit). Growth far beyond income, after allowing for revaluation of land already owned, is a lead.
2. **Follow the goods**: where a physical or licence trail must match the money (ore mined vs royalty paid vs exported, sand permits vs revenue, liquor licences vs excise collected), compare the two. This is how the Lokayukta proved the Bellary mining case in 2011.
3. **Match two records that should agree**: budget released vs spent, works paid vs works done, a minister's statement vs the auditor's finding. Most CAG findings on this site come from this test.
4. **Put everything in writing**: every lead, check and reply is kept on file with its source, so findings survive pressure and can be handed to the authorities intact.

Investigators also seize records, summon witnesses and use insiders. We have none of those powers, so anything that needs them goes to the authorities as a documented complaint.
