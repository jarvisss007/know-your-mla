## The flow, stage by stage

### 1. Budget to department
The legislature votes grants (Appropriation Act). The Finance Department releases money to departments through Khajane-II, Karnataka's treasury system; works departments have historically drawn through Letters of Credit.
Audited (SFAR 2024-25):
- Spending above what the legislature approved: Rs 4,388.75 cr in 2024-25 under three grants (incl. Gruha Lakshmi and free farm power), plus Rs 5,175.21 cr from 2020-21, 2022-23 and 2023-24 still not regularised (para 2.5.2).
- Rs 10,035.13 cr spent through executive orders before legislative approval.
- Of Rs 16,547 cr surrendered, Rs 10,448 cr (63%) was surrendered on 31 March 2025 (para 2.5.5).
- March rush: 35.64% of the year's spending in the last quarter, 18.94% (Rs 72,958.85 cr) in March 2025, 2.62% on 31 March alone (para 2.5.8). SFAR 2023-24 measured March 2024 differently; do not compare.
Records: budget and Appropriation Accounts are public; release orders are inside Khajane-II.

### 2. Year-end parking: deposit accounts and corporation bank accounts
Money moved out of the voted budget into Personal Deposit (PD) accounts or into corporations' bank accounts counts as "spent" but stays spendable.
Audited:
- Rs 12,926.77 cr moved into PD accounts in 2024-25, "includes Rs 6,026.22 crore transferred to PD Accounts in March 2025"; closing balance Rs 29,770.45 cr in 97 accounts (of which Rs 22,115.31 cr is the court-ordered mining-restoration fund); 25 accounts inoperative over three years (SFAR 2024-25, para 3.5).
- Social Welfare moved Rs 145 cr from a vacant-posts provision into two institutions' PD accounts on 30 March 2025; CAG: "mere parking of funds to avoid lapse of grants" (para 2.7.1.2A).
- Rs 760.25 cr released to 20 corporations in the last quarter with instructions to put it in PD accounts (para 2.5.8.1).
- Four test-checked corporations held Rs 412 cr of grants "in the bank accounts of the Corporations outside the Government Account" though shown as spent (para 2.7.1.2B).
- Backward Classes corporations kept 32 bank accounts (Rs 410.43 cr) open despite a February 2023 order to close them (para 2.7.1.5).
- Interest of Rs 14,549.91 cr earned on government funds in 2020-21 to 2023-24 by 64 institutions, companies and corporations was not remitted to government (SFAR 2023-24, para 4.2).

### 3. Drawing cash before the vouchers exist (AC bills)
Officers may draw on Abstract Contingent bills and must file detailed bills by the 15th of the next month.
Audited (SFAR 2024-25, para 3.4): 1,111 AC bills worth Rs 63.90 cr unadjusted at 31 March 2025; 2,797 bills (Rs 275.80 cr) drawn in 2024-25, 185 (Rs 12.80 cr) in March; largest: Elections Rs 32.10 cr, Police Rs 18.37 cr. At March 2024: 934 bills, Rs 45.58 cr.

### 4. Works: estimate, tender, measurement, bill
KTPP Act requires open tender; Section 4(g) lets government exempt notified procurements (KRIDL, others). Payment depends on the measurement book and bills.
Audited (KRIDL PA, 2016-21): 99 of 1,379 test-checked works entrusted above the Rs 2 cr exemption limit (Rs 593.51 cr); 12 works worth Rs 92.43 cr split into 85 so that "EAs preferred KRIDL to bypass the tender process"; event stages and sound systems among ineligible works (Rs 16.28 cr); "Payment vouchers were not supported by valid MB recordings/check measurement"; Rs 303.76 cr received without work codes; funds piled up to Rs 17,320.30 cr by 2020-21.
CA 7/2024 (Haj Ghar, Bengaluru): items worth Rs 2.46 cr "certified as executed were found not executed" on joint inspection.
Cases: BBMP fake bills 2008-11 (about Rs 1,500 cr of works; special court acquitted three accused including MLA Munirathna, April 2026). RR Nagar works through KRIDL, Rs 118 cr (Lokayukta inquiry; engineers suspended; reported, primary not read). "40% commission": the Justice Nagamohan Das commission (report March 2025) found no clear evidence of a 40% rate but "some truth" to commission claims and widespread tender irregularities (ALLEGED; https://thesouthfirst.com/karnataka/40-percent-commission-no-clear-evidence-says-report-but-names-ex-bjp-mlas/).
Records: tender notice and award (public on the procurement portal); estimate, measurement book, bills (RTI).

### 5. Central schemes
Since 2021 central-scheme money sits in Single Nodal Agency (SNA) bank accounts; since 2023 SNA-SPARSH releases it "just in time" from RBI. Transfers to SNA accounts are booked as expenditure though not spent (SFAR 2023-24, ch. 3). In 2024-25 the state took back Rs 1,333 cr of unspent SNA balances (SFAR 2024-25). GoI also sent Rs 22,308.76 cr directly to implementing agencies in 2023-24, outside the state budget (SFAR 2023-24, para 4.4).
Utilisation certificates: 142 UCs (Rs 239.45 cr) outstanding at 31 March 2025 (para 3.3); but UC monitoring covers grants to bodies that account for 8.9% of grants: "around 90 per cent of the grants-in-aid organisations are out of ambit of monitoring" (SFAR 2023-24).

### 6. Beneficiaries (DBT, pensions, rations)
Karnataka pays benefits through its State DBT platform into Aadhaar-linked accounts (NeGD directory). Leak point: the beneficiary list.
- 3.58 lakh bogus pension beneficiaries identified, Rs 430 cr a year saved (Revenue Minister, May 2022). 11.8 lakh "potentially fraudulent" pension beneficiaries flagged by Aadhaar age mismatch, including 13,702 income-tax payers (Revenue Minister, Jul 2025; preliminary).
- Bhovi Development Corporation (CHARGED): about Rs 97 cr paid as loans/subsidy into fake beneficiary accounts; ED prosecution complaint June 2025.
- KIADB Dharwad double compensation (CHARGED): the same land compensated twice; ED found 29 transactions worth Rs 64.48 cr.

### 7. Panchayats and MGNREGA
MGNREGA wages go directly to workers' accounts (NeFMS); materials and works go through the panchayat. Leak points: fake job cards, muster rolls, materials bills.
- Devadurga taluk, Raichur: social audit interim report, about Rs 200.87 cr of irregularities in 33 gram panchayats (2020-23), incl. Rs 102.32 cr paid to one firm in 6,700 cases (PROVEN as social-audit finding; FIR Nov 2023).
- Statewide 2024-25: misappropriation Rs 41.58 cr found, Rs 3.38 cr (8%) recovered (MoRD data as reported).

### 8. After the leak: who catches it, what happens
- Misappropriation, losses and theft: 41 cases worth Rs 149.83 cr pending at 31 March 2025; 14 over ten years old; one HESCOM case is Rs 114.64 cr (SFAR 2024-25, para 3.12).
- Audit questions unanswered: 7,680 inspection reports with 41,193 paragraphs pending against 48 departments at 31 March 2022, 3,142 of them over ten years old (CA 7/2024, Table 1.4).
- The state's replies to SFAR 2022-23 and 2023-24 were still awaited (SFAR 2024-25, para 3.13).

## Fairness notes
- Most audited items are failures of control (money parked, unaccounted, spent without approval), not proof of theft. Say "parked", "not accounted for", "spent without approval" exactly as the auditor does.
- Cases are allegations until a court decides. The one verdict found in these cases is an acquittal.
- Findings cover governments of both parties (KRIDL audit 2016-21; 40% commission inquiry 2019-23; SFAR 2024-25).
