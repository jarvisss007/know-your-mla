How far is California?

California's economy was about USD 4.30 trillion in 2025; Karnataka's about USD 371 billion in 2025-26, so California is about 11.6 times larger, and about 20 times richer per person. The gap closes only if Karnataka grows faster than California for a long time.

Per-person income: Karnataka about USD 5,400 (2025-26), California about USD 109,200 (2025), at the 2025-26 average exchange rate of Rs 88.37 to the dollar. Real growth, fixed exchange rate; an illustration, not a forecast. Karnataka's economy grew 8.1% in real terms in 2025-26 (whole economy, not per person).

The Legislature votes the budget

Every rupee the government spends must first be approved by the Assembly, grant by grant.

Rs 10,035 crreleased through 118 executive orders before the Legislature approved it, across 21 grants (2024-25). The CAG says this list is "only illustrative".CAG Report No. 2 of 2026, para 2.5.1.1

Finance releases, the treasury pays

The Finance Department releases each grant in instalments. Departments' paying officers draw bills on the state's online treasury, Khajane-II, which pays by bank transfer.

Rs 10,093 crspent on 31 March 2025 alone; Rs 72,959 cr, almost a fifth of the year's spending, in March. On the same last day, orders moving Rs 33,106 cr between budget lines were issued under all 28 grants.CAG Report No. 2 of 2026, paras 2.5.8 and 2.8
Rs 7,588 crbooked under the catch-all head "Other Expenditure", which hides what the money was for; Rs 2,533 cr of it had a proper head available.CAG Report No. 2 of 2026, para 3.6

"Spent" on paper, parked in practice

Money moved into deposit accounts or a corporation's bank account counts as spent in the budget, even if nothing has been bought yet.

Rs 29,770 crsitting in Personal Deposit accounts at March 2025, of which Rs 22,115 cr is a mining restoration fund held under a Supreme Court order. Rs 6,026 cr was moved in during March 2025. The CAG: Retention of such large sums of money was against principle of Legislative financial control.CAG Report No. 2 of 2026, para 3.5
Rs 760 crreleased to 20 welfare corporations in the last quarter of 2024-25, and Rs 412 cr remained in the bank accounts of the Corporations outside the Government Account in-spite of being shown as expended.CAG Report No. 2 of 2026, paras 2.5.8.1 and 2.7.1.2

Money that goes around the budget

Some money never passes through the state's main account: central funds sent straight to agencies, and loans taken by state-owned bodies that the state repays.

Rs 22,309 crsent by the Centre directly to implementing agencies in 2023-24, bypassing the Consolidated Fund of the State, so it does not appear in the state's own accounts.CAG Report No. 4 of 2025, para 4.4
Rs 14,155 crof borrowing outside the budget outstanding at March 2025, plus Rs 45,889 cr of state guarantees on others' loans.CAG Report No. 2 of 2026, paras 3.1 and 1.5.6

Works: tender, measure, bill

An engineer estimates the work, it is approved and tendered on the state's procurement portal, the contractor builds, an engineer records the work in a measurement book, and the bill is paid. Small works below a set limit need no open tender.

Rs 28 crin the rural jobs scheme: 13 panchayats split 65 works into 463 smaller ones to stay under approval limits, spending Rs 28.17 cr without the required sanction.CAG Report No. 13 of 2025, para 5.6
Rs 181 crof excess benefit to the contractors on the Yettinahole water project, where dues were not recovered in four packages.CAG Report No. 10 (Yettinahole), para 3.2.2.2
Rs 5,066 crof capital locked in 4,328 projects that were never finished.CAG Report No. 2 of 2026, para 1.6.2

The books are matched

Treasury, division offices, banks and the Accountant General should agree to the rupee. Advances drawn in a hurry must be followed by detailed bills with receipts.

Rs 965 crof public works payments not matched between division offices and the treasury. The CAG warns these differences could lead to non-detection of cases of defalcation.CAG Report No. 2 of 2026, para 3.7
Rs 64 crdrawn on 1,111 advance bills still without detailed bills and receipts; the CAG calls this fraught with the risk of misappropriation.CAG Report No. 2 of 2026, para 3.4

On the ground: the worker, the farmer

In the rural jobs scheme (MGNREGA), villagers check the work in public meetings called social audits, and wrongly paid money must be recovered.

Rs 187 crof misappropriation found by social audits from 2019-20 to 2023-24 (79,284 cases). Rs 90 cr of it was settled without any recovery, and the claimed 94% recovery rate was incorrect and misleading.CAG Report No. 13 of 2025, para 6.4.8
Rs 8,264 cr, nearly half of the scheme's spending in those years, could not be checked by social audit for want of records.CAG Report No. 13 of 2025, para 6.4.7

After the audit

Departments must answer the CAG's objections, and the Legislature's Public Accounts Committee examines the worst cases.

44,174audit objections in 8,522 inspection reports were outstanding against 47 departments at March 2023. The state's replies to the last two State Finances reports are still "awaited".CAG Report No. 6 of 2025, para 1.10.1; Report No. 2 of 2026, para 3.13

These are findings about how the system works and about departments, not proof that any person stole money. Several cover years under governments of both parties: the jobs-scheme audit covers 2019-20 to 2023-24, and the Yettinahole project began in 2012.
