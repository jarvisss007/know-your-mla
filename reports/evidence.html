What you can do

Six things any citizen can do, starting this week. None needs a lawyer.

  1. Ask for the record (RTI)

    Any citizen can ask any government office for its records. A Karnataka application costs ₹10 (free for families below the poverty line); the office must reply within 30 days. If it does not, or refuses, appeal to the officer's senior within 30 days, then to the Karnataka Information Commission within 90 days. Seven ready letters, in Kannada and English, are on our RTI page.

    RTI letters

  2. Complain to the Lokayukta

    If you have evidence that a public servant took a bribe or misused office, any person may complain to the Lokayukta, on its form, with a sworn affidavit. Complain only about what you can show: a knowingly false complaint is itself an offence. If someone demands a bribe, tell the Lokayukta police; they can lay a trap, as in most cases on our tracker.

    See the cases

  3. Ask why permission to prosecute is stuck

    The law asks a department to decide a request to prosecute within three months, plus one month if it needs legal advice (Prevention of Corruption Act, Section 19), and the Supreme Court has held that time limit binding. Ask your department, by RTI, which requests are waiting and since when.

    RTI letter 2

  4. Make your MLA or MLC ask

    Any legislator can put a written question to the government, and the answer is published on the Assembly or Council website. Ask yours to ask: how many sanction requests are pending, which bills are oldest, what happened after a raid in your district.

    Assembly and Council answers

  5. Go to the Gram Sabha and the social audit

    Rural job works must be checked at a social audit in the village; the new rural jobs law that replaced MGNREGA in July 2026 keeps social audits compulsory. Ask to see the work, the photo and the payment. If a work on the list does not exist, say so on the record.

    One tank, many works

  6. Share the evidence, not the anger

    Every figure here has a document behind it. Send the page or the leaflet, not a rumour: a fact with a source is harder to dismiss.
