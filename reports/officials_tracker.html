Gandaberunda · Karnataka officials · reports read up to 24 Nov 2026

After the raid

74 Karnataka officials were raided, trapped, searched, arrested, suspended or charged on 19 days between 17 Jan 2026 and 24 Nov 2026, in the reports read so far. This page follows each one from that first day towards a verdict.

A raid or an arrest is an allegation, not a finding of guilt. Asset figures are the agency's first-day estimates. The page gives the post, not the person: names are in the linked reports and are added here only after the FIR and the person's reply are checked.

Each number counts officials whose case reached that step or a later one, as far as the reports read show. A zero means no report of that step was found. It does not mean the step has not happened: the follow-up check on 24 Nov 2026 was a web search by name in English and Kannada. It could not reach Prajavani, Deccan Herald or the Lokayukta's own website, and did not cover court case-status sites. Every official in the table has been checked at least once. 10 rows carry a later development of another kind (a court order, further searches, a posting, a separate case); each is shown in the table with its source.

How such cases usually end

The state's own figures for corruption cases, all agencies, as reported to the National Crime Records Bureau for 2023 (the latest year published). They show what the cases above are up against.

  1. 362new corruption cases registered in Karnataka in 2023 (245 bribery traps, 84 assets cases)
  2. 195cases charge-sheeted in 2023; 1,448 were still under investigation at the year's end
  3. 120trials finished in 2023, out of 1,690 cases waiting for trial
  4. 35ended in conviction: 29.2%, against 46.2% for India. 74 ended in acquittal

At the end of 2023, 1,529 Karnataka corruption cases were pending trial, 90.5% of all cases due for trial that year. At the 2023 pace of 120 finished trials a year, that is about 13 years of work (our arithmetic). That year courts also quashed 28 cases and stayed 70. Of the people involved, 43 were convicted and 4 were dismissed or removed from service. Source: NCRB, Crime in India 2023, Vol. II, Tables 8C.2 to 8C.5 (pp. 779-784); Karnataka rows checked against the page images on 18 Nov 2026. These are all corruption cases in the state, not only the officials on this page.

Waiting for permission to prosecute

Before a court can try a public servant for corruption, the government that employs them must approve the prosecution (Prevention of Corruption Act, 1988, Section 19). This is where cases stop. How many requests are waiting today is not published.

  • Asked in the Assembly, no answer on the record. On 25 August 2026 two MLAs asked how many prosecution sanctions were pending, for how long, and which officers retired while waiting (unstarred questions 1558 and 1652, Department of Personnel and Administrative Reforms). On 23 November 2026 neither answer was on the Assembly's website (question list of 25 Aug 2026).
  • The last counts come from the Lokayukta, as reported in the press. In January 2024 the Lokayukta's Registrar wrote to the Chief Secretary that 156 cases in 22 departments were awaiting sanction, the most in Rural Development and Panchayat Raj (36) and Revenue (26), one since February 2018 (Siasat, 3 Jan 2024). In September 2024 a Lokayukta official put it at 206 officials in 27 departments (PTI, Oct 2024).
  • Senior officers. Of 195 Lokayukta cases against IAS, IPS, KAS and other senior officers from September 2022 to February 2026, none had ended in conviction or acquittal, and 89 were still under investigation (written reply of then Chief Minister Siddaramaiah, Assembly question 1324, 10 March 2026, read in the original).

Any citizen can ask the government for today's list, department by department: RTI letter 2.
