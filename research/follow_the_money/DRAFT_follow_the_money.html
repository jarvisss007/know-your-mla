Gandaberunda · How Karnataka's money travels · audit figures to March 2025

Follow the rupee

A rupee in Karnataka's budget passes through six or seven hands before it reaches a road, a worker or a widow's pension. At each hand-over a record is made, and a check is supposed to stop misuse. This page follows the rupee stage by stage, shows where the state's own auditor found it parked, unaccounted for or paid for work not done, and names the record that lets anyone trace it back.

Figures marked Audited are from the Comptroller and Auditor General (CAG), quoted from the report itself. Cases are marked Finding (court, audit or social-audit finding), Charged (chargesheet filed, no verdict) or Alleged. Most audit findings are failures of control, not proof that anyone stole: the auditor's words are used as written.

The journey

  1. 1

    The legislature votes it; the Finance Department releases it

    Money sits in the state's Consolidated Fund. The Finance Department releases it to departments through Khajane-II, the state treasury system. Record: budget and Appropriation Accounts (public); release orders (inside the treasury).

    Where control slips Audited

    • ₹4,389 crore spent in 2024-25 above what the legislature approved, under three grants including Gruha Lakshmi and free farm power; ₹5,175 crore from earlier years still not regularised.
    • ₹10,035 crore spent through executive orders before the legislature approved it.
    • ₹14,155 crore owed through state companies and authorities outside the budget on 31 March 2025, ₹5,438 crore of it borrowed in 2024-25. The state repays these loans, but they do not appear in the budget as its debt (para 3.1).
    • Of ₹16,547 crore given back unspent, ₹10,448 crore (63%) was surrendered on the last day of the year, 31 March 2025.

    CAG State Finances Audit Report 2024-25, paras 2.5.2, 2.5.5, 3.1

    Trace it back: the Appropriation Accounts, grant by grant, are published every year by the CAG.

  2. 2

    Year-end parking: deposit accounts and corporation bank accounts

    Money moved out of the budget into a Personal Deposit account, or into a corporation's bank account, is booked as "spent". It is not spent: it is waiting, outside the legislature's yearly control. Record: transfer entries in the treasury; the corporation's own bank statements (not public).

    Where it was parked Audited

    • ₹12,927 crore moved into deposit accounts in 2024-25, including ₹6,026 crore in March 2025. Balance ₹29,770 crore in 97 accounts (₹22,115 crore of it is the court-ordered mining-restoration fund); 25 accounts unused for over three years.
    • On 30 March 2025 Social Welfare moved ₹145 crore meant for vacant posts into two bodies' deposit accounts. The auditor: "mere parking of funds to avoid lapse of grants."
    • Four corporations held ₹412 crore of grants "in the bank accounts of the Corporations outside the Government Account" while the books showed it spent. Backward Classes corporations kept 32 bank accounts holding ₹410 crore open despite a 2023 order to close them.
    • Interest earned on government money and not paid back: the auditor lists 64 bodies and a total of ₹14,550 crore for 2020-24. But ₹11,850 crore of that total is put against the Karnataka Biodiversity Board, which cannot be right as printed: the board received about ₹2.5 crore in all up to 2023 (Deccan Herald), and it has not submitted its own accounts for 2023-24 or 2024-25 (CAG SFAR 2024-25, Table 3.14). Leaving that line out, about ₹2,700 crore, led by Karnataka Neeravari Nigam (₹1,265 crore), KRIDL (₹834 crore), Vishweshwaraya Jala Nigam (₹144 crore) and the Valmiki corporation (₹108 crore).

    CAG SFAR 2024-25, paras 3.5, 2.7.1.2, 2.7.1.5; SFAR 2023-24, para 4.2 and Appendix 4.1; Deccan Herald on the Biodiversity Board

    Audited The interest rule, and how it was kept

    Since a Finance Department order of July 2003, bodies holding government money must send the interest it earns back to the treasury, and "the interest earned shall not be used for any expenditure by the institutions" (repeated in August 2019). The auditor's list of interest not sent back grew from ₹12.82 crore (2021-22 report) and ₹198.99 crore across 13 bodies (2022-23) to about ₹2,700 crore without the Biodiversity Board line (2023-24), the first year the irrigation corporation KNNL and KRIDL appear on it. KRIDL kept unused scheme money in flexi deposits and did not credit ₹238.89 crore of interest earned in 2018-21. In October 2024 the state told its companies to hand over 30% of their bank savings as a "special dividend", and collected ₹970.02 crore in 2024-25, against ₹284.88 crore the year before. The 2024-25 audit does not say whether the unpaid interest itself was recovered.

    CAG SFAR 2021-22 (Report 2 of 2023), para 4.2; SFAR 2022-23 (Report 1 of 2024); SFAR 2023-24, para 4.2; KRIDL performance audit, para 2.3; SFAR 2024-25, chapter 1 and dividend policy

    Charged Valmiki ST Development Corporation, 2024

    This is the stage where the money left. According to the Enforcement Directorate, the corporation's account was moved to a new bank branch in February 2024; ₹187 crore was pooled there between March and May; about ₹90 crore went on to 18 accounts in Hyderabad and through an overdraft taken against the corporation's own fixed deposit. It came out when an accounts official died in May 2024 and left a note. Investigators rebuilt the trail from account-opening papers and bank transfer records. Chargesheets have been filed; there is no verdict.

    South First, Oct 2024 · South First, Jun 2026

    Trace it back: board resolutions approving each bank account, and the bank statements of the corporation. The state told a CAG conference in 2025 it has no central register of such accounts (reported; not read in full). The corporations are traced one by one below.

  3. 3

    Cash drawn before the bills exist

    An officer may draw money on an "abstract contingent" bill for urgent payments, and must file the detailed bill with vouchers by the 15th of the next month. Record: the detailed bill (internal).

    Where the vouchers never came Audited

    • 1,111 advance bills worth ₹63.90 crore had no detailed bill on 31 March 2025; 638 of them date from earlier years. Largest: Elections ₹32.10 crore, Police ₹18.37 crore.

    CAG SFAR 2024-25, para 3.4

    Trace it back: ask the department for the detailed bills and vouchers against each advance.

  4. 4

    Works: estimate, tender, measurement, bill

    A road or building is estimated, sanctioned, put to open tender (Karnataka Transparency in Public Procurement Act, 1999), measured by the engineer in a measurement book, and paid bill by bill. Section 4(g) of the Act lets the government exempt chosen bodies, such as KRIDL, from tendering. Record: tender and award (public portal); measurement book and bills (RTI).

    Where the checks were skipped Audited

    • KRIDL, 2016-21: 99 works worth ₹594 crore handed over above the ₹2 crore exemption limit; 12 works worth ₹92 crore split into 85, the auditor finding departments "preferred KRIDL to bypass the tender process"; event stages and sound systems paid as "works".
    • "Payment vouchers were not supported by valid MB recordings/check measurement": money paid without the engineer's measurement.
    • Money piled up with KRIDL: ₹17,320 crore by 2020-21, ₹304 crore of it received with no work assigned.
    • Haj Ghar, Bengaluru: items worth ₹2.46 crore "certified as executed were found not executed" when auditors visited.

    CAG Performance Audit of KRIDL (Report 9 of 2022); CAG Compliance Audit, Report 7 of 2024, para 2.5.2

    Finding Bengaluru fake bills

    About ₹1,500 crore of city works in 2008-11 were paid on bills for work not done or done poorly, found by the corporation's technical vigilance cell and the CID. In April 2026 a special court acquitted three accused, including a sitting MLA. Separately a Lokayukta inquiry found ₹118 crore paid for Rajarajeshwari Nagar works routed through KRIDL that were not done or less than 30% complete (as reported).

    Udayavani, Apr 2026

    Alleged "40% commission" on contractors' bills

    The contractors' association alleged a 40% cut on bills paid in 2019-23. The Justice Nagamohan Das inquiry (March 2025) found no clear evidence of a 40% rate, but "some truth" to commission claims and widespread tender irregularities.

    South First, Apr 2025

    Trace it back: the measurement book against the road itself. Pages, dates and measurements can be checked on the ground, and photographs dated.

  5. 5

    Central schemes: money in a nodal bank account

    Since 2021, money for centrally sponsored schemes sits in a Single Nodal Agency bank account; since 2023 the SNA-SPARSH system pays suppliers "just in time" from the Reserve Bank. Record: PFMS reports; utilisation certificates.

    Where spending is only on paper Audited

    • Transfers into nodal accounts are booked as spending even though the money is still in the bank; in 2024-25 the state took back ₹1,333 crore of unspent balances.
    • ₹22,309 crore went from the Centre straight to implementing agencies in 2023-24, outside the state budget.
    • Utilisation certificates: 142 worth ₹239 crore outstanding in March 2025, but the system covers bodies getting only 8.9% of grants: "around 90 per cent of the grants-in-aid organisations are out of ambit of monitoring."

    CAG SFAR 2023-24, paras 4.4, 4.5; SFAR 2024-25, para 3.3

    Trace it back: the utilisation certificate and the nodal account statement for the scheme.

  6. 6

    Beneficiaries: pensions, rations, subsidies

    Benefits are paid through the state's direct-transfer platform into Aadhaar-linked bank accounts. The weak point is the list of who gets paid. Record: beneficiary lists (partly public; panchayat notice boards, scheme portals).

    Who should not have been on the list

    • 3.58 lakh bogus pension beneficiaries removed, ₹430 crore a year saved (Revenue Minister, May 2022). In July 2025, 11.8 lakh pensions were flagged as possibly fraudulent, including 13,702 income-tax payers (minister's preliminary count).
    Finding Same bank account, many "beneficiaries" (CAG, 2026)
    • Sports cash awards, 2018-24: money recorded as paid to 62 different beneficiaries was "credited to only three bank accounts", of a person who was not a beneficiary: ₹0.44 crore, which the auditor calls a suspected fraudulent transaction. A further ₹4.54 crore went to 316 sportspersons who did not meet the rules, and vouchers for ₹8.46 crore of ₹11.47 crore in awards were not produced. The government set up an inquiry committee (September 2025).
    • Workers' health insurance (ESIS), Bagalkote dispensary, 2020-24: 902 fake claims were paid on 57 forged sanction orders and 65 more claims were inserted; private hospitals confirmed that bills attached to vouchers were forged. Suspected misappropriation ₹4.03 crore; recovery of ₹2.15 crore decided.
    • Higher education: ₹146.23 crore of government grants kept going to two engineering colleges after they became part of private universities in 2015-16, which made them ineligible.

    CAG Compliance Audit Report No. 1 of 2026 (tabled 26 March 2026), paras 2.1.2.6-2.1.2.8 (pp. 20-24), 2.3 (pp. 55-57), 2.4 (p. 57): report page

    Gruha Lakshmi: what is official so far

    The government itself has said ₹115 crore was credited to 1,08,755 beneficiaries who had died, and that ₹15.24 crore has been recovered (as reported by ThePrint). Larger figures in the press, such as 19,020 beneficiaries sharing bank accounts, come from preliminary audit observations that have not been published; the audit office has said they are not final conclusions. This page will add the auditor's figures when a report is tabled.

    ThePrint

    Charged Bhovi Development Corporation

    About ₹97 crore of loans and subsidies paid into accounts of people who were not real beneficiaries, according to the Enforcement Directorate, which filed a prosecution complaint in June 2025.

    Charged KIADB, Dharwad: land paid for twice

    The same land compensated twice to false claimants: 29 payments worth ₹64.5 crore, according to the Enforcement Directorate.

    Trace it back: the beneficiary list against death, income-tax and land records; that is how the government itself found the bogus pensions.

  7. 7

    The village: panchayats and job-guarantee work

    Job-guarantee wages go straight to workers' accounts; materials and works go through the gram panchayat. Record: job cards, muster rolls, work files, social-audit reports (public at the gram sabha).

    Finding Devadurga taluk, Raichur

    A social audit of 33 gram panchayats (2020-23) found about ₹200.87 crore of irregularities, including ₹102 crore paid to one firm in 6,700 cases and job cards made for people who did not live there (interim report; FIR filed November 2023). Statewide in 2024-25, ₹41.58 crore of misappropriation was found and ₹3.38 crore (8%) recovered.

    South First, Dec 2023 · Ministry of Rural Development data as reported, Mar 2025

    Trace it back: the muster roll against the people named on it, and the work against the site. Any villager can attend the social audit.

5. ₹412 crore in corporations the auditor's company list does not include

Three of the four corporations holding the ₹412 crore of unused grants (Vokkaliga ₹141 crore, Kadugolla ₹49 crore, Maratha ₹30.20 crore) do not appear in the auditor's list of state companies under its audit at 31 March 2023, so there are no audited accounts in that report to check their balances against. The report does not say why; they may be newer, or audited some other way.

Questions the records raise

1. ₹352 crore in one small corporation's accounts

The Nijasharana Ambigara Chowdaiah Development Corporation, set up in November 2017 for the Ambiga, Bestha, Gangamata, Koli, Mogaveera and related communities, was given ₹5 crore in 2024-25. The auditor's table shows ₹190.52 crore in an "escrow" account and ₹159.40 crore in a "recovery" account at Bank of Baroda.

We traced it as far as public records go. Nothing public explains that much money:

  • the corporation spent about ₹32 crore in its first four years and was allocated ₹10.75 crore for 2021-22 (Vijayavani, January 2022);
  • its paid-up capital is ₹12 crore, it has no registered loans, and the auditor found nothing to change in its 2021-22 accounts;
  • no government order, legislature answer, news report or investigation mentions any other money it holds;
  • the same escrow and recovery accounts at three other corporations each hold ₹30 lakh to ₹1.2 crore, and its escrow account number is next to the Uppara corporation's, which holds ₹50 lakh;
  • all three of its balances are round to the lakh, while most others in the table are exact to the rupee.

The auditor's total of ₹410.43 crore includes these figures, so they are not a typing slip in the total. Either the corporation holds money from a source no public record shows, or the department's figures were entered in the wrong unit (₹1.9 crore and ₹1.6 crore would fit its size). Its own balance sheet for 2023-24 or 2024-25, filed with the Registrar of Companies, would settle it; so would an RTI reply to letter 5.

CAG SFAR 2024-25, Appendices 2.15 and 2.16; CAG report on state companies (Report 1 of 2025), Appendices 4-6; company registration CIN U93090KA2017SGC107814; Vijayavani, 21 Jan 2022

2. ₹412 crore "in the bank", but not in the bank accounts listed

The auditor says the four corporations' ₹412 crore of unused grants sits in their bank accounts. Its own list of their bank accounts shows ₹5.24 crore for three of them, and no account at all for the Kadugolla corporation. The report does not say where the rest is held: fixed deposits, treasury deposit accounts, or other accounts.

3. New money while old money is under investigation

The Bhovi corporation, where the Enforcement Directorate alleges about ₹97 crore went to fake beneficiaries (prosecution complaint June 2025), received all of its ₹55 crore for 2024-25 in the last quarter of the year and still ran four bank accounts. The Valmiki corporation, where about ₹90 crore was diverted in 2024 (chargesheets filed), received ₹63 crore of its ₹84 crore for 2024-25 in the last quarter, and had earned ₹108 crore of interest on government money in 2020-23 that it had not paid back.

CAG SFAR 2024-25, Appendix 2.15; SFAR 2023-24, Appendix 4.1. Cases: stages 2 and 6 above.

4. Money released in the last quarter, to be parked

In the last quarter of 2024-25 three welfare departments released ₹760.25 crore to 20 corporations with instructions to "draw the grants within 10 days and deposit the amount into their PD accounts". The Social Welfare corporations received all of their year's grants, ₹425 crore, in that quarter.

CAG SFAR 2024-25, para 2.5.8.1 and Appendix 2.15

Ask for it: the RTI letter for a corporation's bank accounts asks for every account with its balance, the board resolutions that opened them, what the escrow and recovery accounts hold and where the money came from, interest earned and paid back, and the latest audited accounts.

Where the books themselves stop matching Audited

Before anyone can say money leaked, the accounts have to add up. In 2024-25 these did not.

  • Land compensation waiting in deposit accounts. Four Special Land Acquisition Officers held ₹2,087 crore in deposit accounts on 31 March 2025 (SCR Project ₹1,035.80 crore, Kabini Reservoir ₹602.37 crore, Chitradurga ₹283.06 crore, Mandya ₹166.04 crore), and Assistant Commissioners under the Revenue Department another ₹332.73 crore. Land compensation is where the KIADB double-payment case happened (stage 6).
  • ₹1,911.92 crore spent that, on paper, was not there. Deposit accounts showed negative balances in the Accountant General's books. The state replied in January 2026 that it could not close unused deposit accounts because its treasury figures and the Accountant General's do not reconcile. Three-quarters of the total, ₹1,432.81 crore, is a single account in the name of the "Secondary Karnataka Consumer Protection Board", which looks like a booking error rather than real spending; next come "Private Estate under Government Management" (₹262.40 crore) and seven land acquisition accounts (₹138.82 crore paid out beyond what they received, including Hemavathy Reservoir, Hassan ₹43.18 crore and Hemavathy Canal Zone, Tumakuru ₹34.68 crore).
  • Deposit accounts nobody has used for years. Sixteen inoperative accounts still hold ₹339.85 crore: ₹197.41 crore with the Commissioner of Collegiate Education untouched since 2018-19, ₹59.98 crore with the Rural Road Development Agency since 2019-20, ₹50.63 crore with a Special Deputy Commissioner since 2018-19. Another inoperative entry, "Receipts awaiting transfers", carries a debit of ₹5,759.92 crore since 2020-21.
  • ₹7,588 crore booked as "other expenditure". This catch-all head is meant for rare cases because it hides what the money was for. ₹2,532.53 crore went there although a proper head existed, mostly central-scheme spending.
  • Public works payments not yet matched. The "Public Works Remittances" suspense balance rose from ₹82.61 crore to ₹964.68 crore in one year: payments made by works divisions that the accounts have not yet tied to a head.

CAG SFAR 2024-25, Table 3.8, para 3.5 (b), para 3.6 and Table 3.9, para 3.7 and Table 3.11 (pp. 98-100); Appendices 3.1 and 3.2 (pp. 157-158)

After the leak: who catches it, and what happens

  • 41 cases of misappropriation, losses and theft worth ₹149.83 crore were pending in March 2025; 14 of them are more than ten years old (CAG SFAR 2024-25, para 3.12).
  • 7,680 audit inspection reports with 41,193 questions were unanswered by 48 departments in March 2022; 3,142 of the reports were more than ten years old (CAG Report 7 of 2024).
  • The government had not replied to the CAG's state finance reports for 2022-23 and 2023-24 (SFAR 2024-25, para 3.13). Across all audits, 44,526 audit paragraphs were awaiting answers in March 2024, 30,476 of them for more than five years (CAG Report 1 of 2026, para 1.10.1).
  • Recovery is the weakest link: 8% of job-guarantee misappropriation recovered in 2024-25; in the cases on this page, the one verdict found is an acquittal. See also After the raid.

What you can ask for

Every stage above leaves a record. Any citizen can ask for it under the Right to Information Act: the deposit-account balance of a department, the bank accounts of a corporation, the detailed bills against an advance, the measurement book of a road in your ward, the beneficiary list of your panchayat. Ready-made letters for raid follow-ups are on the RTI page.

Read this fairly

  • Parking money, spending ahead of approval and missing paperwork are failures of control. They make theft easier to hide; they are not proof of theft. This page uses the auditor's own words.
  • Cases marked Charged or Alleged are not proven. Courts have not ruled on most of them.
  • The findings span governments of both main parties: the KRIDL audit covers 2016-21, the commission inquiry 2019-23, and the state finance audit 2024-25.
  • Gaps: how Letters of Credit work today inside the treasury system, and which schemes use the new "just in time" payments, were not confirmed from primary documents.
