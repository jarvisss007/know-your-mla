DRAFT · NOT PUBLISHED Rural job scheme (MGNREGA) · Koppal and Ballari · what was promised, what it could mean

What if it had all been built?

The government's own records list 246 water works at two village tanks, with ₹13.05 crore booked against them. Each one is a canal, pond, bund or check dam that a village was promised. The photos show two tanks. Here is what that money could mean for the people who live there, why nobody's checks stopped it, and how to fix it.

Based on One tank, many works. We do not know that anyone took this money: wages are paid into workers' own bank accounts, and real digging did happen at both tanks. The question is whether ₹13 crore bought 246 works, or two tanks booked 246 times.

What ₹13.05 crore is, in a village

3,738households given the full 100 days of guaranteed work, at the 2024-25 Karnataka wage of ₹349 a day
3,311farm ponds, at the median cost recorded for farm ponds in Karatagi and Kuknoor (₹39,408)
3,530cattle sheds, at the median recorded cost (₹36,957)

Each figure is the whole ₹13.05 crore spent one way, to give a sense of scale. Median costs come from the scheme's own public records for 2,082 works on farmers' land in Karatagi and Kuknoor taluks.

What the paper promised, and what the photos show

On paper: Kampli, 7 panchayats

  • 69 canals and channels to carry water from the tank to fields and to divert floods
  • 43 bunds and embankments to hold soil and water on slopes
  • 40 ponds to store rain for the dry months

In the photos: one tank

All 152 completion photos show the same tank near Kanavi Timmalapura, dry in April 2024 and full in November. Sharper satellite images show the tank bed was cleared between 2019 and 2022, but no new canals, channels or separate ponds around it.

On paper: Jeerala, 11 panchayats

  • 23 check dams to slow streams and recharge wells
  • 23 ponds, including fisheries ponds that could earn an income
  • 24 bunds and embankments, 14 trenches, 10 spurs

In the photos: one tank bed

All 94 completion photos were taken from one roadside spot by the Jeerala tank. The bed was dug into pits and trenches between 2016 and 2022, and that work was real. What the photos cannot show is 23 check dams and 23 separate ponds spread across 11 panchayats.

What that would mean on the ground. A pond or check dam in each of 18 panchayats means wells that last longer into summer, a second crop where there is now one, and fewer families leaving for city work after the harvest. Canals from a full tank carry water to fields that today depend on rain. If the 246 works were built where the records say, those villages have them. If they were not, the records say they do, and nobody will build them again.

Why doesn't anyone stop it?

Every rupee passes five checks. At each one, the check that exists is not the check that is needed:

Panchayat plans the workGapOne tank can be split into dozens of work codes across panchayats, and nothing flags it.
Engineer measures itGapMeasurement books are paper, rarely public, and not compared across works at the same site.
Photo proves completionGapAny photo at any spot is accepted: 152 works at one tank, 153 works at one GPS point.
Moderator accepts the photoGapKarnataka rejected 419 of 42.5 lakh photos, 0.010%. Duplicates of one object passed.
Social audit and OmbudspersonGapThey act on complaints and samples. Without data like this, nobody knows where to look.

So the system does not fail because nobody is watching. It fails because each person checks only their own step, and no one compares across steps. The pattern only shows up when you look at all the records together, which is what this reporting did using public data and free tools.

How to fix it

Changes the government can make, using data it already has

1. Flag one site, many works

Automatically hold payment when five or more work codes from different panchayats are photographed within 50 metres, until an engineer certifies separate structures.

2. Reject frozen locations

Refuse a geotag whose location exactly matches a different work's photo taken on another day. It cannot be right.

3. Check photos against each other

A simple image fingerprint, the kind used here, finds the same object submitted for two works. It costs almost nothing to run on every upload.

4. Photos inside the panchayat

Flag any completion photo taken more than 10 km from the work's own panchayat. Gangavathi and Karatagi lead the state on this.

5. Publish the measurement books

Put the measured quantities online beside each work code, as muster rolls already are, so anyone can add them up for one tank.

6. Audit by risk

Send the social audit first to the blocks at the top of the statewide ranking, not to a random sample.

What you can do

  1. Check your block in the statewide ranking, and look up any work code on nrega.nic.in to see its muster rolls and payments.
  2. Go to the Gram Sabha and the social audit meeting in your panchayat. Ask to see the measurement book for any work near you, and walk to the site.
  3. File an RTI. Our full texts are free to copy: The paper trail. The fee is Rs 10.
  4. Complain to the MGNREGA Ombudsperson of your district, who must hear complaints about the scheme. Our requests for Koppal and Ballari are in the paper trail.
  5. Ask your MLA to raise it in the Legislative Assembly and at the taluk development review meetings.

Can it be fixed?

Yes. Every check proposed here uses data the government already collects: locations, photos and work codes. The analysis behind these pages took days, not years, using free public tools. If one independent publication can find the pattern, the department that owns the data can stop it before the money is paid.
