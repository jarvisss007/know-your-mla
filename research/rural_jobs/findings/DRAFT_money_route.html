From Delhi to a worker's account

How a rupee of the rural job scheme travelled from the Union budget to the bank account of the person who dug the pond, who signed off at each step, which record it left behind, and where our reporting found that step failing. Every record named here can be asked for under the Right to Information Act.

Based on the scheme's guidelines, the state auditor's performance audit of the scheme in Karnataka (Report No. 13 of 2025), answers given in the Legislative Assembly and Lok Sabha, and our own reading of 8.24 lakh public completion records. From 1 July 2026 a new law replaced the scheme, with the state paying 40 per cent instead of about 10; the works described here were all under the old rules.

If wages go straight to the worker, how does money go missing?

A worker's wage cannot be taken from the bank on the way. The ways known to auditors and the scheme's own guidelines all work earlier or around the side:

  • Job cards that are not real workers, or cards held by someone else who withdraws the money.
  • Days paid that were not worked, with part of the wage handed back.
  • Machines doing the digging while the muster roll shows people.
  • Material bills, which go to suppliers and are the easiest to inflate.
  • One work booked as many. Each work code can be paid in full; if one tank carries fifty codes, the earthwork can be measured fifty times.

Each of these leaves a record: a bank credit, a muster roll, a measurement, a bill. The public portal we read shows only steps 2, 4 and 10. The rest are in the files the letters on this site ask for.

Which letter to send

  • A work near you looks unfinished or missing: ask about one work (steps 4 to 10).
  • Many works seem to be the same place: ask about one site (steps 2, 4 and 6).
  • People in your village lost their job cards: ask about deleted job cards (steps 3 and 8).
  • You want to know who checks the photos: ask how photos are checked (step 10).
