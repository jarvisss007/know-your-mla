    Five MLAs tested

    Five affidavits were read line by line across elections, and every figure was re-derived by an independent check. None of this shows wrongdoing. These are the facts that held up.

    K. Gopalaiah

    BJP · Mahalakshmi Layout

    • About Rs 30 crore of property bought from November 2018 to November 2022, on about Rs 7.4 crore of declared income. New loans cover the difference.
    • His declared income rose from Rs 16.84 lakh to Rs 3.47 crore in one year. No reason is given.

    Satish Jarkiholi

    Congress · Yemkanmardi

    • About nine-tenths of his rise from Rs 42.5 crore to Rs 175.3 crore is higher values on land held before 2018.
    • One plot is listed as 10 acres, inherited, in three affidavits and as 57 acres, bought for Rs 28,000, in 2023.

    Munirathna

    BJP · Rajarajeshwarinagar

    • 19 properties costing about Rs 25 crore bought between June 2018 and September 2020.
    • A corruption case declared in 2018 does not appear in his 2020 or 2023 affidavits. Its outcome could not be found.

    N.A. Haris

    Congress · Shantinagar

    • Nearly all of his rise from Rs 190 crore to Rs 439 crore is revaluation of property bought before 2018.
    • He chairs Bengaluru's development authority while his family holds city land bought between 1990 and 2006. No decision about that land was found.

    Priyakrishna

    Congress · Govindarajanagar

    • He has bought no property since 2013.
    • Rs 780.88 crore of his liabilities sits in one line of creditors that the archive does not name.

    For journalists

    Everything on this site is free to use, with or without credit. Each story below rests on public records linked on this page, and the full dataset can be downloaded.

    India's tech capital has no elected council

    Bengaluru · both governments

    • No elected city council since September 2020.
    • The Supreme Court moved its election deadline three times in 2026; it now stands at 31 December.
    • The city's annual development grant rose from Rs 3,000 crore to Rs 7,000 crore in that time.

    One term, double the wealth

    224 MLAs · every party

    • Among 81 re-elected MLAs, average declared assets rose from Rs 39.7 crore to Rs 69.4 crore between 2018 and 2023.
    • 36 of the 81 at least doubled their declared wealth.
    • From the MLAs' own sworn affidavits; much of the growth is land revaluation, which the data lets you check per MLA.

    195 cases, zero verdicts

    Karnataka Lokayukta

    • Of 195 raid and trap cases against officials since September 2022, none had reached a conviction or acquittal by February 2026.
    • 62 were stayed by the High Court.
    • 156 cases awaited the government's sanction to prosecute in August 2023.

    They doubled their own pay, twice

    Legislative Assembly · both parties

    • MLAs' basic salary went from Rs 25,000 to Rs 40,000 in 2022 and to Rs 80,000 in March 2025, both times without discussion.
    • 32,870 of 69,915 Health Department posts were vacant in December 2024.
    • The Assembly has sat fewer than the 60 days its own law requires in every year since 2016.

    How to read this

    • Every figure is self-declared by the MLA in a sworn affidavit and archived by the Association for Democratic Reforms at myneta.info. Nobody audits these figures.
    • A declared case is not a conviction. Counts include protest and election-code cases alongside corruption cases. Open the affidavit to see the charges.
    • Declared wealth is not evidence of wrongdoing. Growth often reflects land being revalued, not new income.
    • Figures are from the 2023 election. Some seats have changed hands since. Asset figures were not available for 14 MLAs.
    • Growth since 2018 is shown only for re-elected MLAs who could be matched across both elections.

    About Gandaberunda

    Gandaberunda is an independent, citizen-run publication that holds Karnataka's elected representatives to the public record. It is named after the two-headed bird of Karnataka's history, which looks both ways at once, as this work looks at every party. It is not affiliated with the Government of Karnataka or any political party, and does not use the official state emblem.

    Standards

    • Every figure links to its source, preferably a primary record: an affidavit, an audit report, a budget or a court order.
    • Allegations are attributed to whoever made them, and the person's denial is reported where one exists.
    • Nobody is called guilty unless a court has convicted them.
    • Every party gets the same scrutiny. When a case against someone is quashed or ends in acquittal, that is reported too.
    • No coverage of anyone's private life, or of family members who are not accused.
    • Research is assisted by AI tools. Every figure is checked against its source before it is published.

    Independence

    • Gandaberunda takes no money from any party, candidate or government, and carries no advertising.
