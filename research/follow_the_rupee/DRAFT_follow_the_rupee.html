Backtracking one trail: Bengaluru white-topping

White-topping lays concrete over a tar road. The state has announced this programme several times since 2023. We followed it from the announcement down to the payments the corporation itself publishes.

What the gap could mean. Three readings fit, and the records we have cannot choose between them: the work is far behind; the work is done but contractors are unpaid (the Deputy CM put pending contractor bills across the state at about ₹37,000 crore in March 2026); or large packages are paid through accounts OpenCity's files do not cover (OpenCity has said its data leaves out some large projects). Each has a document that settles it, listed at the end.

Where the trail goes dark

  • At the corporation's cash desk. Of ₹11,693 crore in bills (2024-25 and 2025-26), 87% does not say which grant paid for it. About 10% says only "GoK grants"; under 3% names a scheme (Amrut Nagarothana ₹191 cr, Nava Nagarotthana ₹93 cr). From the public record, most rupees cannot be tied back to the budget line they came from.
  • Inside the treasury. Khajane-2 records every bill and payee, but has no public bill-level view.
  • Between the order and the payment. Measurement books, running bills and quality reports are where "paid but not done" shows up. None is published.

Two things found while digging (leads, not findings)

  • Consultancy bills pile up just under ₹5 lakh. 231 bills sit between ₹4.75 and ₹5 lakh, 16 just above. Some single projects were billed in same-day pieces, such as a road survey for one ₹50 crore package paid as two bills of exactly ₹4,95,000. The procurement law's no-tender limit for services, as written in 2001, is ₹1 lakh. An RTI for the award records is drafted.
  • 2,972 bills appear twice with new payment numbers. Almost all cluster around a restart of payment numbering in November 2024, which points to a records change rather than double payment. The ₹2,919 crore total must not be quoted. Questions to OpenCity and the accounts department are drafted.
